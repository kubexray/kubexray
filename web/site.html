The flight recorder for your Kubernetes cluster.

kubexray records every change to your cluster. When something breaks, it shows you the change that likely caused it, who made it, and what to run next. Then share the whole incident as a replay anyone can watch, without access to your cluster.

Every rollout, image, scale and config edit, who made it, and whether it settled healthy.

Caused by

Every incident comes with its likely cause

Dashboards tell you a pod is crashing. kubexray tells you why, and what changed just before.

  • What's wrong, in plain English, with the command to run next
  • The likely cause, who made it, and the evidence for it
  • One cause with many symptoms is one incident, not twelve alerts

Change feed

Every change, and what it did

One line per Helm upgrade, Argo CD sync or kubectl edit, with a verdict once things settle.

  • Healthy settled, and how long it took
  • Broke checkout and when it was over
  • Fixed it brought an app back
  • Drift hand edits to managed objects

Rewind

Go back to the moment before it broke

kubexray records around the clock. Drag the timeline to any moment and the whole cluster is drawn as it was. Compare two moments to see exactly what changed in between.

In your terminal

Ask why, get an answer

kubexray why gives the same diagnosis without opening a browser: impact, symptom, likely cause, evidence, and what to run next.

Built to run in production

Read-only and private

It only lists and watches. Secret and ConfigMap contents are never read, and replays are scrubbed.

Alerts with the cause attached

An Alertmanager receiver that forwards to Slack or a webhook with the likely cause and a link to the moment it started.

Sign in with your company accounts

Built-in OpenID Connect for Google, Okta, Entra ID and Keycloak, with an allow-list and an audit log.

History that survives restarts

Seven days on a volume by default, with a disk budget. The incident from 3 a.m. is still there in the morning.

Fast at scale

Every incident and its cause found in 1 ms on 1,000 pods. The full map renders in about 80 ms.

Post-mortems drafted for you

A Markdown timeline of what changed, what broke and when it recovered, linking to the replay.

Install

In your cluster

Records around the clock behind authentication. Open it with a port-forward, or put it behind an Ingress with sign-in.

helm install kubexray oci://ghcr.io/kubexray/charts/kubexray -n kubexray --create-namespace
Helm chart values →

On your laptop

One binary that uses your kubeconfig, like kubectl. It prints which cluster it's reading before it connects.

brew install --cask kubexray/tap/kubexray
